## Main findings

- **Broken summaries:** lines 80293, 205957 and 261622 contain only `S:`. They are unusable answers even though nonempty. Line 1338 is otherwise a decent concise summary, with a small satisfaction-to-score wording drift. Scan for marker-only targets separately from semantic review.
- **Grounding:** line 29799 calculates changes correctly but invents an only-variable explanation and causal certainty. Line 5 invents causal links between adherence, recommendation and outcomes. Line 257534 fabricates incident details from an ambiguous center/IP report. Line 4756 adds an erosion limitation absent its passage. These exemplify plausible-sounding elaboration beyond context.
- **Evidence calibration:** line 54116 establishes a descriptively larger score drop, not statistical certainty of superiority. Its short-horizon caveat is useful but incomplete. This is evaluation of a supplied synthetic passage, not medical advice or an externally verified clinical claim.
- **Numerics:** line 54058 is correct under equal rates; line 312719 silently uses cost markup instead of selling-price margin (648 versus 675 TL including VAT). Line 211417 overstates a climate-rainfall ratio and omits requested comparison details.
- **Task inference versus editing:** 13442, 132798 and 249481 are raw sentences without a correction request; 53466 adds a Soru/Cevap wrapper, still no explicit editing task. Corrections are often sensible, so ambiguity is not proof the model behavior is wrong. Their training signal nevertheless teaches unsolicited proofreading from ordinary utterances. For an intentional editing subset, add an explicit instruction with provenance-preserving transformation; for general chat, assess likely conversational intent before using these targets. Line 54115 contains an actual spelling question and benefits from a direct answer plus minimal corrections, rather than repeating the corrected question. No hidden system editing instruction resolves this ambiguity.
- **Grammar quality:** corrections coexist with misleading rule explanations (13442, 132798), incomplete root analysis (313475), and obligation drift across registers (313783). Line 173494 provides a plausible detailed morphology contrast. Parentheses left open in 132798 are a presentation defect, not invalid JSON.
- **Format and completeness:** JSON cases pass syntax and grounding checks; list/table examples generally preserve records. Line 154043 misses required transition words despite five steps. Line 133694 exposes a system/user conflict: user requests a recommendation while system says table-only, yet target adds prose outside table. A recommendation row could satisfy both.
- **Coverage/style:** sampled work includes summaries, grounded QA/analysis, arithmetic, spelling/morphology, register rewrites, extraction/normalization, decisions, technical/product plans, simplification and one safe refusal. Repeated five-step plans with bold headings and Gerekce clauses (125551, 154043, 314788), S/C scaffolding and generic pro/con advice suggest low template diversity. Similar structure is not automatically duplication or contradiction. Broad technical necessities and formulaic certainty deserve review.

## Translation and benchmark-like questions

Initial broad lexical scan found 4,082 prompts matching çevir/tercüme, but inspected hits included table conversion (8255), a translator credential in a job application (82851), and plain-language rewriting (83348). These three full-text probes inform coverage only and are not included in the 42-row JSONL. They do not establish interlanguage translation coverage. A narrower corpus scan for İngilizceye/İngilizceden/Almancaya/Fransızcaya/translate/translation or Türkçeye tercüme produced one candidate, 257534, a false positive from İngilizce Yeterlilik. Regex dots can match spaces. Therefore **no actual interlanguage translation task was confirmed**, not a claim of absence; broader language/direction classifiers would be needed.

A scan for hangisidir, seçeneklerden or line-start A-option formatting found 189 lexical candidates. First three were fully inspected (176, 4756, 5116): passage QA or open technical advice, not identifiable benchmark items. Arithmetic word problems (54058, 312719) are benchmark-like in form; that is a leakage screening cue, not proof of origin. PISA mention in 116898 is passage subject matter, not itself an evaluation question match. No canonical/source-ID or cross-lingual benchmark comparison was performed, so contamination remains unresolved. Parent's 700 normalized duplicate-prompt groups / 1,022 excess rows span the full corpus audit; they are not Atlas-only semantic contradictions and were not independently adjudicated here.
