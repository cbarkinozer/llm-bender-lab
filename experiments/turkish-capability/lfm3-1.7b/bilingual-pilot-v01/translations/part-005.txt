My child has the camera on while playing online games with friends; is this a safety risk?
@@ASSISTANT
Short answer: If your child turns the camera on only with friends they know from real life, the risk is low; but if they turn it on in environments that include people they do not know, then yes, this is a risk. The main concerns are:

* Someone they do not know can see your child and their room; location clues can be drawn from details in the background such as a school uniform or the view from the window.
* Their image can be recorded without permission, screenshotted and shared.
* Malicious people can try to build trust through the camera and approach the child.

Practical precautions:
* Turn the camera on only with familiar friends. Make sure the people they video chat with are really people they know; the camera should never be turned on with people they do not know.
* Keep it off by default. Turn it off in the game settings and in the device's camera permissions, and turn it on only when needed.
* Use a camera cover. Fitting a sliding cover to the computer camera is simple and effective.
* Have them play in a shared space. Playing the game in a common area of the home is good for both supervision and trust.
* Talk. Explain, in a non-accusatory way, that they should not share personal information (full name, school, address) and that they should tell you right away if someone bothers them or asks for something strange.

Banning the camera entirely is not necessary; playing with video alongside familiar friends can also be socially positive. What matters is keeping under control who the camera is open to.

Although the contract between the parties contains an arbitration clause, the other party has taken the dispute to a state court. In this case, how should I raise the arbitration objection (preliminary objection), and how should I write the petition?
@@ASSISTANT
If the other party files a lawsuit in a state court in a dispute that has an arbitration clause, the objection to be raised is technically not “lack of jurisdiction” but the preliminary arbitration objection (Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) Art. 116/1-b). Because this is a preliminary objection, it must be raised in the statement of defense and within the time limit for the defense (HMK Art. 117). If it is not raised in time, it will not be heard and the court will continue to hear the case; the court does not take this objection into account on its own motion. If the objection is found well-founded, the case is dismissed on procedural grounds.

Sample petition:

TO THE ... COURT
CASE NO: 20.../...
DEFENDANT: [Full name / Title, address]
COUNSEL: [if any, Att. ...]
PLAINTIFF: [Full name / Title]
SUBJECT: Request that, since there is a valid arbitration clause between the parties, our preliminary arbitration objection be accepted and the case be dismissed on procedural grounds.

EXPLANATIONS
The plaintiff has filed this case with the claim of ...
Article ... of the contract dated ... between the parties contains the following arbitration clause: “[text of the clause]”. This provision is a valid agreement showing that the parties subjected the dispute to arbitration.
Since the dispute falls within the scope of this clause, the place of resolution is arbitration, not the court. We raise our preliminary arbitration objection within the time limit and together with our statement of defense.

LEGAL GROUNDS: HMK Arts. 116, 117 and 407 et seq.; the relevant article of the contract; the agreed arbitration rules.

CONCLUSION AND REQUEST: We respectfully request that our preliminary arbitration objection be accepted, that the case be dismissed on procedural grounds, and that the litigation costs and attorney's fees be borne by the plaintiff.

[Date] — [Defendant / Counsel, signature]
ANNEXES: Copy of the contract, (if any) power of attorney

Note: This is a sample template, not legal advice. Before adapting it to your specific case, have the current legislation and case law checked by a lawyer.
